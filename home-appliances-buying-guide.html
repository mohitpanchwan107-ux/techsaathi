Home Appliances Buying Guide

A practical checklist for comparing TVs, refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners and kitchen appliances.

Before buying: check the exact model number, current price, installation charges, warranty terms and service availability in your area. Features and energy labels can vary by model and year.

1. Televisions: choose for your room and viewing

Measure the viewing distance and available wall or cabinet space before choosing a screen size. Compare resolution (Full HD or 4K), panel type, brightness, viewing angles, refresh rate and HDR support. If you play games, check HDMI 2.1 features, input lag and the supported refresh rate rather than relying only on marketing labels.

Also check the smart-TV operating system, app availability, number of HDMI ports, sound quality and software-update policy. A soundbar may improve dialogue, but include its cost in your total budget.

2. Refrigerators: capacity, layout and electricity use

Choose capacity based on household size, shopping habits and kitchen space—not just the largest model you can afford. Measure doorways and the final installation space, including ventilation clearance. Compare single-door, double-door, side-by-side and convertible layouts based on how you store food.

Read the current BEE energy label for the exact model and label period. Compare annual energy consumption where available, compressor warranty, total product warranty, stabilizer guidance and local service support. Do not assume that every inverter or convertible feature saves the same amount of electricity.

3. Washing machines: type, capacity and real needs

Compare semi-automatic, fully automatic top-load and front-load machines. Capacity should suit laundry volume and bulky items such as bedding. Check water pressure requirements, wash programmes, spin speed, noise, dimensions, installation needs and whether your household prefers simpler controls or more specialised cycles.

Look closely at the warranty on the complete machine versus the motor, as these can differ. Ask whether installation, inlet pipes, stand and any required accessories are included or charged separately.

4. Air conditioners: sizing and installation

AC capacity depends on room area, ceiling height, sunlight, insulation, occupants and local climate. Use a reputable sizing assessment rather than choosing only by room size. Compare inverter operation, rated cooling capacity, ISEER/energy label information, noise, filters and operating-temperature range.

Installation can materially affect performance and cost. Confirm copper-pipe length included, extra piping charges, outdoor-unit placement, electrical requirements, drainage and warranty conditions before paying.

5. Kitchen appliances: safety and daily usability

For microwaves, kettles, mixer-grinders, induction cooktops and dishwashers, compare usable capacity, power requirements, safety protections, cleaning effort, noise and spare-part availability. For induction cooktops, check cookware compatibility. For dishwashers, check dimensions, water connection, loading layout and detergent availability.

Read the manual for ventilation and electrical requirements. Never overload a socket or ignore grounding requirements for high-power appliances.

6. Compare the full cost, not just the sticker price

  • Product price and delivery fee.
  • Installation, accessories and old-appliance removal.
  • Expected electricity or water consumption.
  • Warranty duration and what is excluded.
  • Service-centre access and spare-part availability.
  • Return, replacement and damage-on-delivery rules.

7. Verify the exact model before checkout

Similar product names can refer to different capacities, finishes, model years or feature sets. Match the complete model number across the manufacturer's official page, energy label and seller listing. Confirm seller identity, invoice, delivery terms and the current final price before ordering.

Quick comparison checklist

  • Does it fit the room, doorway and available connections?
  • Are capacity and core features suitable for everyday use?
  • Have you checked the exact-model energy label where relevant?
  • Are installation and ongoing running costs included in your budget?
  • Are warranty terms and local service clear?
  • Have you compared at least two alternatives from reliable sellers?

Nivoget provides general buying information, not laboratory testing or a guarantee of product performance. Specifications, prices, availability and warranty terms can change. Verify details with the manufacturer and seller before purchase.
