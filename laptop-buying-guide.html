Laptop Buying Guide: What Actually Matters?

A practical checklist for students, office work, creators and everyday users.

1. Start with your real workload

For browsing, documents and online classes, a modern entry-level laptop may be enough. Video editing, software development and demanding games need stronger processors, more memory and sometimes a dedicated graphics processor. Choose for your actual tasks, not just the biggest specification.

2. Check the processor generation and model

Compare the exact CPU model and generation, then look for independent reviews of that specific laptop. A processor's name alone does not tell the whole story because cooling and power limits also affect performance.

3. Choose enough RAM

For general use, 8GB can be a tight minimum; 16GB is a more flexible target for many Windows laptops. Heavy creative work can need more. Check whether RAM is upgradeable or soldered before buying.

4. Check SSD storage and upgrade options

An SSD generally makes a laptop feel more responsive than a traditional hard drive. Estimate how much space your apps, files and videos need, and verify whether the storage can be upgraded.

5. Inspect the screen and keyboard

Check resolution, brightness, reflections, colour quality and refresh rate. If possible, try the keyboard and touchpad. Screen size and weight matter if you carry the laptop every day.

6. Battery, ports and connectivity

Read battery-life tests for the exact configuration. Check USB-A, USB-C, HDMI, headphone and charging needs, plus Wi-Fi and Bluetooth versions. Some thin laptops require adapters.

7. Warranty, repair and total cost

Check the warranty, local service options, return policy and included charger. Add the cost of any required adapters, software or accessories to the budget.

Before buying: Compare at least two or three exact configurations. Specifications and included components vary by model and region. This is general buying advice, not a hands-on product test.
